Every handover between them is re-typing, and every re-typing is a place the number changes. So the questions that decide the business get answered late, or by feel:

→ Did MH 12 AB 1234 make money last month, or did diesel and tyres eat it?

→ Which of my lanes is quietly running at a loss?

→ How many trucks stood idle this week, and whose?

The books close six weeks after the month does. By the time a bad lane shows up in the P&L, you have already run it forty more times.

What Phloz is

One software for everything a fleet operator does — from the indent to the ledger entry.

Phloz is a fleet ERP built for Indian transport operations. Demand capture, dispatch planning, trip execution, ePOD, billing, driver settlements, maintenance, compliance and double-entry accounting are not integrations. They are the same system, writing to the same rows.

Entered once

The trip is the record

A consignment booked in operations is the same object that gets tracked, proved, invoiced and posted. Nobody re-keys it into accounts.

Priced from the start

Costed before it moves

Rate card, lane distance, fuel surcharge and GST resolve at booking — so the margin on a load is known before a driver is assigned to it.

Closed the same day

Books that keep up

Invoices, vendor bills, trip expenses and fuel purchases post themselves to a real double-entry ledger as they happen.

End-to-end workflow

Indent to receipt, without re-typing anything.

One unbroken chain. Each step inherits everything the last one knew, and each step leaves an audit trail behind it.

Planning

Tomorrow's plan, costed before the trucks move.

The dispatcher loads the day's demand and gets back a feasible, priced plan instead of a whiteboard. The solver respects capacity, temperature class, body type and time windows — and prices the decision every operator makes a hundred times a month: run it on my own truck, or hire it out?

01  Collect — pull tomorrow's indents and open orders into one plan

02  Solve — route own dry, own reefer and attached capacity against a costed objective

03  Commit — one action turns the plan into real orders, trips and hire requirements

Anything the solver can't serve profitably in-house surfaces as a hire requirement you can send out for quotes, rather than disappearing.

Driver management

Advances, bhatta and settlements — closed to the rupee, not the diary.

The driver's account is a ledger, not a page in the munshi's notebook. Advances released on dispatch, on-road spends captured against the trip, and the settlement that nets them off at the end of it.

Driver master

The whole file

Licence and its expiry, Aadhaar, police verification, reporting branch and current trip status — one record per driver.

Money on the road

Advance & expense

Cash released on dispatch, then toll, bhatta, loading, unloading, parking, halting, RTO and police spends booked against the trip.

Settlement

Netted, then posted

Trip closes, expenses net against the advance, the balance is payable or recoverable — and the ledger entry writes itself.

Accountability

Issues from the cab

Breakdowns, tyre failures, accidents and check-post delays reported against the vehicle and the trip they happened on.

Expenses the driver paid credit driver advances; expenses the office paid credit cash — the distinction the books need, captured at entry

Asset management

No truck leaves the yard on an expired fitness certificate.

Every vehicle carries its papers, its service history and its running costs in one record. Statutory documents sit on a renewal watchlist; preventive maintenance is scheduled by odometer and calendar, so a service is due when the truck says so, not when someone remembers.

Papers tracked: RC, insurance, fitness, national and state permits, PUC, road tax, FASTag KYC, GPS certificate

Per vehicle: diesel and mileage, tyres, maintenance jobs, on-road expenses, loan and EMI head

Fleet shapes: own, attached, leased and hired — grouped into fleets, optionally owned by a vendor

Profitability

P&L at every level you actually manage — not just the one you file.

A company-level P&L tells you the year went well. It never tells you which truck, which lane or which customer paid for it. Because every operational document posts to the ledger with a cost centre on it, the same books answer all six questions.

Real double entry, not a summary table. Every financial event becomes a balanced journal entry, and the reports are derived from those lines — which is what keeps the books consistent when eleven people are entering data at once.

Built for India

Freight here doesn't move like freight anywhere else. Neither does Phloz.

Not a global fleet product with a rupee symbol bolted on. The rules Indian transport actually runs on are modelled in the data, not worked around in a comments field.
